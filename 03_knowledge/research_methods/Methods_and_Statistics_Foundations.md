# Research Methods and Statistics Foundations

## Status and use

This is a plain-language preparatory guide. It does not approve a methodology, replace a methods text, or authorise data collection. A current brief, ethics requirements and supervisor direction take priority.

## The research chain

```text
Question -> concept/theory -> design -> data -> analysis -> result -> bounded conclusion
```

If one link is weak, the conclusion must be narrower.

## 1. Quantitative, qualitative and mixed methods

| Approach | Plain Chinese | Often useful for | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quantitative | 用數字檢查變數之間的模式 | prevalence, association, comparison, prediction | treating correlation as causation |
| Qualitative | 用訪談、文件或觀察理解意義與過程 | how, why, experience, context, process | claiming broad generalisation from a narrow sample |
| Mixed methods | 讓數字和深度解釋互相補強 | questions needing both pattern and explanation | doing two studies without integrating them |

## 2. Variables and measurement

**Variable（變數）:** something that varies, such as employee turnover intention or training hours.

**Construct（構念）:** an abstract idea, such as organisational commitment, which usually needs several indicators to measure it.

**Operationalisation（操作化）:** the decision about how an abstract construct becomes observable data.

**Check:** Does the measure actually represent the construct, or only a convenient proxy?

## 3. Sampling

**Population（母體）:** the group you want to say something about.

**Sample（樣本）:** the people/cases actually studied.

**Sampling frame（抽樣框）:** the list or route from which the sample is drawn.

**Bias（偏誤）:** a systematic reason why the sample may differ from the target population.

**Plain rule:** a large sample cannot repair a badly defined population or a biased route into the sample.

## 4. Correlation is not causation

If two variables move together, at least four explanations remain possible:

1. X influences Y.
2. Y influences X (reverse causality).
3. A third factor influences both.
4. The pattern is due to measurement, selection or chance.

Use wording such as “is associated with” unless the design supports a stronger causal claim.

## 5. Reading common statistical outputs

**Mean:** average value. It can be distorted by extreme values.

**Median:** middle value. It is often more robust when data are skewed.

**Standard deviation:** how spread out values are.

**Confidence interval:** a range expressing uncertainty around an estimate under the model's assumptions.

**p-value:** how surprising data at least this extreme would be if a specified null model were true. It is not the probability that the hypothesis is true, nor evidence of practical importance.

**Effect size:** how large a relationship or difference is. Report it alongside uncertainty where possible.

## 6. Qualitative analysis: a safe starting frame

1. Preserve source details and context.
2. Familiarise yourself with the data.
3. Create initial codes grounded in the data and your question.
4. Compare cases, including negative or deviant cases.
5. Develop themes with clear boundaries and supporting extracts.
6. Keep an audit trail: how codes and interpretations changed.
7. State the researcher's position and the limits of transferability.

## 7. Validity, reliability and trustworthiness

| Term | Simple meaning | Question to ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Validity | Are we measuring or explaining what we claim? | Could another explanation fit the evidence? |
| Reliability | Would the measurement/process be consistent? | Is the procedure documented and repeatable? |
| Credibility | Is a qualitative interpretation well supported? | Do the data and analysis trail justify this theme? |
| Transferability | Can readers judge relevance elsewhere? | Is the context described clearly enough? |

## Before you analyse any real data

Record the research question, data provenance, consent/ethics status, data cleaning decisions, missing-data treatment, analytic assumptions, software/version and all deviations from the plan. Use [Data Analysis Plan](../../04_templates/research/Data_Analysis_Plan.md) when a project starts.
