# Criticality and Academic English Toolkit

## Purpose

Use this guide to understand, discuss and practise academic ideas. It is not a substitute for an assessment-specific AI policy, rubric or your own authorship. For assessed work, apply the AI-use route before requesting English drafting.

## Criticality: from description to judgement

| Level | What it does | Example move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Description | reports what a source says | “Barney (1991) argues that…” |
| Comparison | identifies agreement/difference | “Unlike X, Y treats…” |
| Evaluation | judges strength under conditions | “This explanation is useful where…, but less persuasive when…” |
| Critique | tests assumptions, evidence and omissions | “The argument assumes…, yet the evidence cannot rule out…” |
| Synthesis | builds a justified position across sources | “Taken together, the studies suggest…, although…” |

Distinction-level work is not “more negative”. It makes a clear, proportionate judgement that follows from evidence and recognises limits.

## The paragraph engine: Claim -> Evidence -> Analysis -> Link

1. **Claim:** make one focused point that answers the question.
2. **Evidence:** use relevant, verified support.
3. **Analysis:** explain why the evidence supports, qualifies or challenges the point.
4. **Link:** show how this point advances the overall argument.

### Useful sentence frames

- “This suggests that ___, particularly where ___.”
- “However, this interpretation rests on the assumption that ___.”
- “A competing explanation is ___, because ___.”
- “The evidence is informative but limited by ___.”
- “Therefore, the managerial implication is conditional: ___.”

## High-value vocabulary

| Term | Plain Chinese | How to use it carefully |
| --- | --- | --- |
| assumption | 預設前提 | identify it, then test whether it holds |
| mechanism | 發生的機制／原因鏈 | explain how X could lead to Y |
| boundary condition | 適用邊界／條件 | specify when a theory may not apply |
| causal inference | 因果推論 | reserve for designs that address alternatives |
| generalisability | 可推廣性 | distinguish statistical from contextual relevance |
| validity | 有效性 | say which validity concern you mean |
| reliability | 穩定性／一致性 | do not confuse with validity |
| synthesis | 綜合不同來源形成觀點 | show relationships, not a list of summaries |
| implication | 含意／實務意涵 | state who should do what, under which conditions |
| limitation | 限制 | explain what it changes about the conclusion |

## Common upgrades

| Too broad | More defensible |
| --- | --- |
| “This proves that CSR improves performance.” | “This evidence is consistent with a possible relationship between CSR and performance, but does not by itself establish causality.” |
| “Culture explains the failure.” | “National-cultural differences may be relevant, but organisational structure, incentives and institutional conditions also require consideration.” |
| “The theory is wrong.” | “The theory has limited explanatory power in this context because…” |

## Self-check before calling something “critical”

- Have I separated the source's claim from my interpretation?
- Have I named an alternative explanation, limitation or counter-evidence?
- Does my judgement follow from evidence rather than stronger language?
- Have I said what changes in practice, for whom, and under what condition?

## Practice routine for a reading

Write four lines in Chinese first:

1. The author's main claim.
2. Why it matters.
3. The strongest limitation.
4. Your provisional judgement.

Then write one English paragraph using the Claim -> Evidence -> Analysis -> Link structure. Keep the source and your judgement visibly separate.
