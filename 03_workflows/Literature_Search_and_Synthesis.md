# Workflow: Literature Search and Synthesis

## Purpose

Build a transparent, reproducible evidence base for coursework or dissertation work without overstating completeness or manufacturing a literature gap.

## Required inputs

- Review or assignment question.
- Scope: concepts, context, population/organisation, geography, period, and source types.
- Available databases, library access, and known seed sources.
- Deadline and expected depth.

If database access is unavailable, produce a search protocol and clearly label any web-based results as provisional.

## Search modes

| Mode | Appropriate use | Permitted claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exploratory scan | learning a new topic, finding vocabulary and seed papers | identifies leads; not comprehensive |
| Structured coursework search | building a defensible evidence base under assignment constraints | transparent and bounded |
| Dissertation review | supporting gap, theory, and design decisions over time | comprehensive within documented scope; not automatically systematic |
| Systematic review | only when the research design and reporting standard explicitly require it | use the applicable formal protocol |

Never label a review `systematic` merely because several databases were searched.

## Method

1. **Frame the question:** define the decision or claim the search must inform.
2. **Build concept blocks:** list synonyms, related terms, spelling variants, and exclusions. Keep the full Boolean strings.
3. **Choose sources:** justify databases, citation indexes, official repositories, and any grey-literature sources.
4. **Run and log searches:** record platform, exact query, filters, date, and result count in `Search_Log.md`.
5. **Deduplicate and screen:** apply predeclared inclusion/exclusion criteria. Record exclusion reasons at full-text stage.
6. **Expand carefully:** use backward and forward citation searching from strong seed papers; label how each source was found.
7. **Verify and extract:** process included sources through `Literature_Input_Pipeline.md` and `Source_Verification_Log.md`.
8. **Synthesize:** compare theories, contexts, methods, findings, contradictions, and limitations in `Literature_Synthesis.md`.
9. **Map to claims:** use `Claim_Evidence_Matrix.md` to identify support, challenge, uncertainty, and missing evidence.
10. **Apply a stop rule:** stop when the predefined scope is covered and another search round adds no material theory, method, contradiction, or high-relevance evidence—or when the documented assignment constraint is reached.

## Literature-gap rule

A gap is not simply `few papers were found`. Classify the proposed gap as one or more of:

- unresolved theoretical contradiction;
- underexamined context or boundary condition;
- methodological weakness;
- inconsistent or insufficient evidence;
- practical problem not adequately explained by existing research.

State what was searched, what was not searched, and how confident the gap claim is. Prefer `not identified within the documented search` over `no research exists`.

## Output

- Search mode and scope.
- Search strings, sources, dates, filters, and counts.
- Inclusion/exclusion decisions and limitations.
- Synthesis of consensus, disagreement, methods, and boundaries.
- Claim–evidence map.
- Qualified gap statement or explicit conclusion that no defensible gap has yet been established.
- Next search or reading action.
