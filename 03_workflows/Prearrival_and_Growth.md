# Workflow: Pre-arrival and Academic Growth

## Purpose

Prepare for the MSc without pretending to know unissued module requirements.

## Task Context

Current capability evidence, pre-arrival timeframe, known programme context, and the student's learning priorities.

## Required Inputs

A self-assessment plus at least one authentic sample or task where practical; official module information only when available.

## Method

1. Record current capability against a target: critical reading, academic writing, literature search, citation management, research design, quantitative literacy, qualitative analysis, presentation, and academic English.
2. Choose one or two priority skills for a four-week practice cycle.
3. Use a small authentic task: analyse one article, create an argument map, practise a Harvard reference, or compare research designs.
4. Record evidence of progress and the next practice target.
5. When official modules arrive, map skills and interests to the actual learning outcomes and assessments.

## Optional career alignment

Relate modules, research interests, and demonstrable skills to possible next steps (industry research, policy, research assistantship, or doctoral study) without treating it as a binding career recommendation.

## Output

Prioritised skill gap, bounded practice cycle, evidence of progress, and next deliberate-practice task.

## Gaps

Record skills based only on self-report, missing writing/research samples, unknown module requirements, and targets that cannot yet be calibrated.

## Next Action

Complete the smallest authentic practice task that produces evidence for the highest-priority skill.
