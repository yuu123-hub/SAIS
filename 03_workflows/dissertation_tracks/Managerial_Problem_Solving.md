# Dissertation Track: Managerial Problem Solving

## Use when

The primary purpose is to analyse a defined management problem and produce evidence-led, context-sensitive recommendations.

## Task Context

A managerial-problem dissertation with a defined stakeholder, decision context, programme rules, supervisor direction, access, confidentiality, and ethics status.

## Required Inputs

Current Dissertation Status, problem owner, decision need, scope, success criteria, relevant literature, feasible evidence, and applicable ethics/confidentiality guidance.

## Method

### Required decisions

1. Client/stakeholder and decision problem
2. Scope, context, constraints, and success criteria
3. Relevant management literature and theoretical framing
4. Evidence plan and research design
5. Ethics, access, confidentiality, and conflicts of interest before data collection
6. Analysis, options, recommendation, implementation implications, risks, and evaluation plan

### Quality checks

- Do not confuse a client preference with a research finding.
- Compare feasible alternatives and their trade-offs.
- Keep recommendations proportionate to available evidence.
- Separate commercially confidential material into ignored project folders.

## Output

Problem-definition record, option/evidence map, recommendation conditions, implementation risks, supervisor questions, and next milestone.

## Gaps

Record unclear client problem, stakeholder conflict, unavailable organisational evidence, weak theoretical framing, confidentiality/ethics risk, or infeasible implementation assumptions.

## Next Action

Resolve the earliest unvalidated problem, evidence, or governance decision.
