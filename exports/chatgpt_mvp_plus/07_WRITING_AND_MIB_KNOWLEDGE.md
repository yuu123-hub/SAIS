## Standard of work expected by this pack

- Answer the exact question and demonstrate the current rubric, not a generic topic overview.
- Make a precise, contestable claim; support it with appropriate theory and verified evidence.
- Explain the warrant: why the evidence supports, qualifies or challenges the claim.
- Evaluate evidence strength, assumptions, alternative explanations, boundaries and practical implications where relevant.
- Make a proportionate, qualified judgement rather than using stronger language to appear critical.
- Use clear signposting and British academic English. Separate fact, interpretation, suggestion and uncertainty.

## Criticality ladder

| Level | Move |
|---|---|
| Description | State what a source, case or concept says. |
| Comparison | Identify meaningful agreement or difference. |
| Evaluation | Judge fit, evidence quality or limitation under stated conditions. |
| Critique | Test assumptions, trade-offs, boundaries and competing accounts. |
| Synthesis | Build a justified position across evidence and theory, with limits. |

## Paragraph engine

`Claim -> Evidence/Theory -> Analysis -> Link`

Before calling a passage critical, ask: Have I distinguished the source claim from my interpretation? Have I named a limitation, alternative or counter-evidence? Does my judgement follow from evidence? What changes in practice, for whom and under what condition?

# Critical Analysis Guide

- SAIS MVP Version: 0.1.0
- Document Status: Export-only attributed composite
- Document Type: Critical analysis guide
- Target Institution: The University of Sheffield
- Target Programme: Management and International Business MSc
- Source Files: `Criticality_and_Academic_English.md`; `Criticality_Model.md`; `POL-020_Criticality.md`; `07_quality_control/Challenger.md`
- Source Versions: SAIS v1.6.0
- Purpose: Turn criticality rules into practical review questions.
- Applicable Tasks: Essay/report analysis; literature review; proposal; dissertation
- Authority Level: Knowledge and quality-control aid
- Known Gaps: Criticality must be task-relevant; it is not a quota of negative sentences.

## Questions to apply to each material claim

1. What must be true for this claim to hold?
2. What theory, mechanism or alternative explanation could also fit?
3. Does the evidence directly support the wording and strength of the claim?
4. What does the method, context, sample or case limit?
5. Is correlation being treated as causation, or a local case as a general rule?
6. What counter-evidence, stakeholder, institutional context or cross-cultural factor matters?
7. What implication follows, for whom, and under what conditions?

## Safe language moves

- `This suggests that ..., particularly where ... .`
- `However, this interpretation rests on the assumption that ... .`
- `A competing explanation is ... because ... .`
- `The evidence is informative but limited by ... .`
- `Therefore, the implication is conditional: ... .`

Do not replace analysis with a list of criticisms. Explain the consequence of each limitation for the conclusion.
