## Select a search mode

| Mode | Use | Permitted claim |
|---|---|---|
| Exploratory scan | Learn vocabulary and find leads | Identifies leads; not comprehensive. |
| Structured coursework search | Build a defensible assignment evidence base | Transparent and bounded. |
| Dissertation review | Support gap, theory and design decisions | Comprehensive within documented scope; not automatically systematic. |
| Systematic review | Only when required by the design and reporting standard | Use the required formal protocol. |

## Method

1. Frame the question or decision the search must inform.
2. Define scope: concepts, context, population/organisation, geography, period and source types.
3. Build concept blocks, synonyms, exclusions and reproducible Boolean strings.
4. Choose and justify databases or repositories; log exact searches, filters, dates and counts.
5. Deduplicate and screen against predeclared inclusion/exclusion criteria; record full-text exclusion reasons.
6. Use citation searching carefully and record how sources were found.
7. Verify and extract each included source.
8. Synthesize by theory, context, method, finding, contradiction and limitation rather than author-by-author summary.
9. Map support, challenge, uncertainty and gaps to claims.
10. Apply a stop rule and state limitations.

## Literature-gap rule

Do not state that no research exists because few papers were found. A defensible gap is an unresolved theoretical contradiction, underexamined context/boundary, methodological weakness, inconsistent/insufficient evidence, or practical problem not adequately explained. Prefer `not identified within the documented search` where confidence is limited.
