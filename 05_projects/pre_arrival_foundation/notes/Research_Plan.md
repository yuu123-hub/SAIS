# Pre-arrival Research Plan

## Task Context

- **Project:** Pre-arrival Foundation
- **Purpose:** Establish a repeatable MSc research, reading, citation, and argument workflow before module-specific requirements are issued.
- **Time horizon:** Four weeks; adjust once official module handbooks, learning outcomes, rubrics, and AI-use guidance are available.
- **Current material:** `materials/harvard.docx`.

## Facts

- The supplied guide states that the University of Sheffield supports an author-date Harvard system based on *Cite Them Right*.
- It distinguishes in-text citations from a complete reference list, requires citation when quoting, paraphrasing, or summarising, and advises page numbers for direct quotations or a specific part of a source.
- The guide advises avoiding secondary referencing where the primary source is available, and notes that personal data may require ethical approval and lecturer or supervisor guidance.
- No current module handbook, assessment brief, marking rubric, reading list, course-specific AI guidance, or supervisor direction has been supplied.

## Analysis

The immediate priority is not to practise a speculative assignment format. It is to build the transferable habits that will support every future module: accurate source recording, distinction between summary and analysis, defensible claims, and traceable referencing. The current source is a referencing guide, not management or international-business evidence; it should therefore govern citation practice but should not be used to support disciplinary claims.

## Four-week plan

### Week 1 — Reference-management baseline

**Objective:** Establish a reliable Harvard workflow.

1. Read the guide's sections on in-text citation, quotation, paraphrasing, summarising, reference lists, journal articles, reports, web pages, and generative AI.
2. Create five practice references from real sources you have personally accessed: one journal article, book or chapter, official report, web page, and lecture or course material if available.
3. For each source, record author, year, title, publication details, DOI/URL, access date where relevant, source type, and a short note on reliability.
4. Write one 150-word paragraph that uses one paraphrase and one short quotation; check that both are cited and that the quotation has a verified page number.

**Deliverables:** five Evidence Records; one revised paragraph; an initial Skill Gap Tracker entry.

### Week 2 — Critical reading and evidence extraction

**Objective:** Turn one peer-reviewed management or international-business article into reusable evidence.

1. Select one article with a stable DOI or publisher URL.
2. Before reading, state a provisional question, for example: *How does [management or international-business phenomenon] affect [outcome] in [context]?*
3. Complete a Reading Note: research question, theory, method, findings, limitation, page-located quotation(s), and potential relevance.
4. Create one Theory Card and one Concept Card from the article; distinguish the author's claims from your interpretation.

**Deliverables:** one Reading Note; one Theory Card; one Concept Card; one Evidence Record.

### Week 3 — Argument construction and challenge

**Objective:** Practise evidence-led, critical academic argument rather than description.

1. Choose one bounded claim that follows from the Week 2 article.
2. Build an Argument Map with: claim, theoretical basis, supporting evidence, limitation, counterargument or alternative explanation, and managerial implication.
3. Draft a 300–400 word mini-analysis in British English.
4. Run the SAIS Auditor and Challenger: identify unsupported assertions, over-generalisation, missing counter-evidence, and citation gaps; revise once.

**Deliverables:** one Argument Map; one mini-analysis; one Auditor/Challenger log.

### Week 4 — Research-design orientation and system calibration

**Objective:** Connect reading and argument skills to a feasible future research question.

1. Turn the Week 3 topic into one exploratory research-question card.
2. Compare two plausible research designs at a high level (for example, interviews/case study versus survey/secondary data): what each could show, its limits, data access needs, and ethical issues.
3. Do not collect data or claim methodological approval; record questions for future handbook and supervisor guidance.
4. Complete the Skill Gap Tracker and write a short reflection: what improved, what remains weak, and the next four-week priority.

**Deliverables:** one Research Question Card; one Methodology Audit; updated Skill Gap Tracker; one Reflection.

## Quality gates

- Do not invent bibliographic details, page numbers, research findings, or module expectations.
- Use supplied or personally verified sources only; keep the original PDF or stable URL with every source record.
- Label interpretation as analysis and keep it separate from verifiable source claims.
- Do not treat a source's journal ranking, recency, or citation count as known unless independently checked.
- Pause and add the official handbook/rubric when received; it overrides this generic plan.
